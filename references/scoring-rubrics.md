# Scoring rubrics

All scores from this skill are practice estimates. Say so whenever a band score is reported, and never present an estimate as a prediction of a real exam result.

## Listening and Reading

Score objective sections as raw correct answers, then convert with the approximate tables below. The tables follow the commonly published IELTS conversions and vary slightly between real test versions.

Listening, raw score out of 40 to band:
39 to 40 is 9.0, 37 to 38 is 8.5, 35 to 36 is 8.0, 32 to 34 is 7.5, 30 to 31 is 7.0, 26 to 29 is 6.5, 23 to 25 is 6.0, 18 to 22 is 5.5, 16 to 17 is 5.0, 13 to 15 is 4.5, 10 to 12 is 4.0.

Academic Reading, raw score out of 40 to band:
39 to 40 is 9.0, 37 to 38 is 8.5, 35 to 36 is 8.0, 33 to 34 is 7.5, 30 to 32 is 7.0, 27 to 29 is 6.5, 23 to 26 is 6.0, 19 to 22 is 5.5, 15 to 18 is 5.0, 13 to 14 is 4.5, 10 to 12 is 4.0.

For a single slice, convert proportionally and report the raw score first, the band second and both as estimates. In the review, name the trap in each missed item, such as a paraphrase, a corrected detail or a distractor that matched the wording but not the meaning.

## Speaking

Score four criteria with equal weight, following the public IELTS band descriptors.

- Fluency and coherence: how steadily the speaker keeps going, how ideas are linked, and how much pausing is spent searching for words rather than ideas.
- Lexical resource: range and precision of vocabulary, collocation and paraphrase.
- Grammatical range and accuracy: mix of sentence forms and how often errors block meaning.
- Pronunciation: judged only approximately from a transcript and recording notes. A transcript shows word choice and self correction but not intonation, so say what could not be judged.

Rough anchors: band 6 speakers hold a conversation with some hesitation and mainly simple sentences used accurately. Band 7 speakers speak at length with flexible vocabulary, frequent collocation and a range of structures with mostly error free sentences. Band 8 speakers are fluent with rare hesitation, wide precise vocabulary and structures that are almost always accurate.

Feedback order matters: report the estimated band, then the two changes that would raise it most, then corrections, then the model answer. Count fillers and repeated words from the transcript as fluency evidence, and note speaking length against the part target.

## Writing

Score four criteria with equal weight, following the public IELTS band descriptors.

- Task achievement for Task 1 and task response for Task 2: whether the task is fully answered, with a clear position in Task 2 and the key features covered and supported in Task 1.
- Coherence and cohesion: paragraphing, progression of ideas and linking devices used without overuse.
- Lexical resource: range, collocation, spelling and word formation.
- Grammatical range and accuracy: sentence variety and error density, weighted by whether errors block meaning.

Check the word minimum before scoring content and report underlength answers plainly. In the review, show the rewritten version at the target band and name the three or four changes that carry most of the improvement, rather than annotating every line.

## Error taxonomy for the log

Tag every logged error with one of these categories so the profile can find patterns: grammar tense, grammar agreement, article, preposition, collocation, vocabulary range, paraphrase miss, detail trap, spelling, coherence, fluency filler, and task response. Log the pattern and one short example, never the whole answer.
